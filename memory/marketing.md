## 1. Brand Positioning

**Elevator pitch.** Broad is the rider's companion — a quiet, considered app for Indian motorcyclists. Plan a route, ride together with your crew, hold for SOS when a day goes sideways, keep your papers in a Glovebox that never leaves your phone. Built for the long road, not the leaderboard.

**Three audience-specific one-liners.**

- **Touring crew** — "From the city limits to the last switchback, your convoy stays on the same map."
- **Weekend rider** — "Plan Saturday's ride on Friday night. Ride it on Saturday. Re-read it on Sunday."
- **Daily commuter** — "RC, insurance, licence — locked to your phone, ready at the next checkpoint."

## 7. Twitter / X Thread Starters

### Thread 1 — Glovebox privacy stance

> Your RC book is on your phone. Not on our server. Not on anyone's cloud. Not in a "secure backup."
>
> Here is why we built Glovebox that way, and why we will never change it. (1/n)

- Tweet 2: The brief from day one — riders kept asking, "where do my docs live?"
- Tweet 3: Every other answer involved an upload. We hated all of them.
- Tweet 4: Glovebox is encrypted on-device. Biometric-locked. Never leaves your phone.
- Tweet 5: This means: lose your phone, lose your Glovebox. We chose that tradeoff. So did our riders.
- Tweet 6: Privacy is not a feature. It's a default. Or it's a lie.

---

### Thread 2 — Why we don't gamify rides

> No streaks. No XP bar. No "Top 10 Riders This Week."
>
> Broad will never have a leaderboard. Here's why a ride app should not feel like a fitness app. (1/n)

- Tweet 2: Leaderboards reward speed. Speed kills riders.
- Tweet 3: Streak mechanics push you to ride when you shouldn't — tired, late, in bad weather.
- Tweet 4: A ride is not a workout. It's a conversation between you, the road, and your crew.
- Tweet 5: We track distance, top speed, highest point. Quietly. For your records, not for ranking.
- Tweet 6: The reward for finishing a ride is the ride. We refuse to insult that with a badge ceremony.

---

### Thread 3 — The design philosophy

> No chrome. No drop shadows. No neon. No emojis in the UI.
>
> Broad is built like a printed agenda, not a video game. A short thread on what that means and why it matters at 90 km/h. (1/n)

- Tweet 2: At speed, the eye reads contrast and weight. Not gradients and glow.
- Tweet 3: Our Live Ride screen is one thing — a 72-point Fraunces serif numeral. Your speed. Nothing else competing for the look-down second.
- Tweet 4: 1px rules instead of shadows. 2px corners. Paper and ink. Amber when something needs you now.
- Tweet 5: The aesthetic is a safety feature. Every pixel earns its place or it gets cut.

---

### Thread 4 — Why we built SOS

> The first thing we built was Plan. The second thing we built was SOS.
>
> Here's the order, and why it matters. (1/n)

- Tweet 2: Most ride apps treat safety as a settings-screen footnote. We treated it as a screen of its own.
- Tweet 3: 1.2 second hold to trigger. Long enough to prevent accidents. Short enough to use one-handed at the side of the road.
- Tweet 4: The screen turns obsidian and pulses. Your crew gets your pin. Your emergency contacts get your pin.
- Tweet 5: The "I am safe" screen is the one we want you to see. Two-second hold. Confirmed. Day continues.
- Tweet 6: We hope you never use SOS. We built it like you might.

### Thread 6 — Why "the docket" not "your trips"

> Most apps call it "Your Trips." We call it "the docket."
>
> A small thing. But naming is half of the design job. (1/n)

- Tweet 2: "Your Trips" is a list. A docket is a record. Something you can hand someone.
- Tweet 3: Every ride in Broad gets filed. Distance, top speed, highest point, crew, the route.
- Tweet 4: It is not a feed. It is not a timeline. It is a log book.
- Tweet 5: Riders have always kept log books. We just gave it a serif and a system.
- Tweet 6: Words shape what the product feels like. We pick them like we pick the type.

## 9. App Store Copy

**App name.** Broad — The Rider's Companion

**Subtitle (max 30 chars).** Plan. Ride. Return. (24 chars)

**Short description (170 chars).**
The companion app for Indian motorcyclists. Plan routes on real maps, ride with your crew, hold for SOS, and keep your papers locked to your phone. Built for the long road.

**Promotional text (170 chars).**
New: Trip Complete card with field-note quotes, Discover feed for open-invite rides, and a Glovebox that never leaves your device. Quiet by design. Loud when it matters.

**Full description (~3000 chars).**

Broad is the rider's companion. A quiet, considered app for Indian motorcyclists who care about the long road, the right route, and the friends they ride with.

It is built around four things.

**Plan.** Plot a ride on a real OpenStreetMap. Search any place in India, drop waypoints, see the live elevation profile, add the names of the riders coming with you, jot down what you are packing. Save it. The whole ride sits in your pocket before the kickstand is up.

**Ride together.** Open Live Ride and the screen turns obsidian. A 72-point serif numeral shows your speed. Top speed, elapsed time, and distance sit underneath, set in the same restrained type. Your convoy appears as a list — every rider's position, speed, and fuel level, updated live over a real-time connection. No leaderboard. No rank. Just the crew, on the same map.

**SOS.** When a day goes sideways, hold the SOS button for 1.2 seconds. The screen turns obsidian and pulses. Your live location goes to your crew and to your emergency contacts at the same instant. When you are okay, hold "I am safe" for two seconds. A confirmation screen appears. The day continues. We hope you never use it. We built it like you might.

**Glovebox.** Your RC book, insurance certificate, driving licence, PUC, and medical information, all locked to your device with biometric authentication. Never uploaded. Never synced. Never on our server. If you lose your phone you lose your Glovebox. We chose that tradeoff on purpose. Privacy is a default, or it is a lie.

**Other things Broad does, quietly.**

- Discover feed of open-invite rides from clubs and crews near you
- Crash detection toggle, background location, push notifications, haptics
- A Trips Archive ("the docket") with Active, Upcoming, and Past tabs styled like a printed journal
- A Trip Complete card after every ride — distance, top speed, highest point, a rotating field-note quote
- A profile that shows lifetime distance, total trips, and the highest point you have ever reached, set in 72-point serif

**The aesthetic is the safety feature.** No chrome. No drop shadows. No neon. No emojis in the UI. At 90 km/h the eye reads contrast and weight. We give it that. Paper and ink. Amber only when something needs you now.

**Built in India, for Indian roads.** Bengaluru to Chikmagalur. Delhi to Spiti. Mumbai to the Konkan coast. Pune to the ghats. Manali to Leh. The Northeast circles. Every map tile, every place name, every waypoint built to feel right when you are riding here.

The rider's companion. For the long road.

## 11. Visual Direction for Content Creators

**Photography style.** Natural light, always. Early morning, late afternoon. Tungsten if you must. No ring light. Allow grain — film grain, sensor noise, low-ISO honesty. Compositions should feel found, not staged. Hands on the tank. A helmet on a fence post. The bike in the corner of a wide landscape, not the centre of it. Roadside chai. The pannier zip half-open. The kickstand digging into gravel. Sunrise over a ghat road. The map on the phone, propped against a fuel tank.

Avoid neon, avoid chrome reflections styled as the hero, avoid the MotoGP knee-down cliché, avoid drone-zoom-and-spin transitions, avoid colour grades that lean teal-and-orange Hollywood. Avoid posed group shots with crossed arms.

If a frame feels like a gym-shoe ad, throw it out. If a frame feels like a page from a printed travel journal, keep it.

**Do / don't.**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Natural light, golden hour, blue hour | Ring lights, studio strobes, neon |
| Film grain, slight imperfection | Over-sharpening, plastic skin |
| Wide compositions, bike in context | Tight low-angle hero shots of chrome |
| Hands, helmets, gloves, papers | Stock-photo "thumbs up" rider poses |
| Roadside, dhabas, fuel pumps, viewpoints | Closed-set racetrack imagery |
| Quiet candid moments | Knee-down sparks, wheelies, burnouts |
| Real riders, real bikes, real wear | Models who clearly do not ride |
| Paper, leather, wood, asphalt, dust | Plastic prop staging, glossy product shots |
| Honest weather — fog, drizzle, glare | Always-blue-sky perfection |
| Fraunces serif text overlays in ink | Display fonts, scripts, neon type |

## 12. Founder / Personal Story Angles

Five hooks the founder can publish on personal LinkedIn or X to seed launch.

**Hook 1 — The origin moment.**
> Three years ago a friend went down on a ghat road outside Sakleshpur. He was alone. His phone was in his tank bag. None of us knew where he was for forty minutes. He is fine now. I started building Broad two weeks later.

**Hook 2 — The privacy decision.**
> The single hardest decision in building Broad was refusing to upload our users' RC books to a server. Every advisor told us to. Every competitor does. Here is why we held the line.

**Hook 3 — The "no leaderboard" stand.**
> The first feature request we ever got was a leaderboard. The second was streaks. We shipped neither. We will never ship either. If you build for riders, you do not turn riding into a workout app.

**Hook 4 — The aesthetic argument.**
> A rider looks at their phone for less than a second at a time. That second decides what features earn their place on the screen. This is why our app does not look like any other ride app on the store. A short thread on the design choices that came out of that constraint.

**Hook 5 — The Indian-roads thesis.**
> The big ride apps were built for German autobahns and California canyons. None of them know what to do with a Tata truck doing 30 in the right lane on NH-48. Broad was built here, by riders who ride here, for the roads we actually have.
